# Deal risks

## Risk patterns

Check the retrieved evidence for each pattern. Missing evidence means unknown. Describe the pattern and the check without copying buyer quotes or identities into the repo.

1. Unmet must-have. A buyer requirement depends on an unavailable capability. Classify each must-have as shipped, roadmap, or unavailable. Roadmap items do not qualify the deal on that requirement.
2. Promise beyond the written position. A rep assurance exceeds approved guidance. Compare security, legal, and roadmap statements with the written position from the responsible team.
3. Buyer misbelief left standing. A buyer relies on an incorrect product or contract assumption. Check whether the correction was provided in writing.
4. Gate owned elsewhere. An external certification, release, or partner controls the decision. Identify the dependency, owner, and date.
5. Extension without conversion. More access delays the decision. Check that each pilot or extension has a dated conversion test agreed by the buyer.
6. Moving usage denominator. Usage proof mixes grants, seat counts, or time windows. Require a fixed window and seat count that the buyer can verify.
7. Records disagree with paper. Seats, amounts, or dates differ across records. Compare Salesforce, the order form, and billing evidence before close, marking unavailable sources unknown.
8. Future capability sold as current. The buyer expects to purchase an unshipped capability. Identify where product vision has become a purchase condition.
9. Incumbent anchor. An existing product sets the comparison. Identify the incumbent and the specific term or capability the buyer expects matched.
10. Internal team unconfirmed. A key meeting depends on people who have not accepted. Check the needed roles and each person's confirmation.
11. Stakeholder shift. The contact or decision maker changes. Re-map decision power and validate the champion after each change.
12. Split deal structure. A separate environment creates separate administration, billing, or agreements. Verify whether the buyer expects one deal or more than one.
13. Champion exit without backup. The deal depends on a departing or silent champion. Check for another active relationship with influence and a path to the economic buyer.
14. Unused pilot. Provisioning is mistaken for adoption. Require evidence of actual use before treating the pilot as qualification or proof of value.
